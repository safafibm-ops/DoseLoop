Every worker's H₂S dose, read from a card on the wrist.

DoseLoop is a reusable wristband that holds a one-time colour-changing sensor card. The worker scans it with a phone at the start and end of each shift, and the app logs that shift's hydrogen sulphide dose in ppm·hr. No battery, nothing to charge.

Exposure limits are a dose. Most workers never get one measured.

H₂S limits are written as an average over a shift, so what matters is the total a worker takes in, per shift and over months. A fixed gas alarm or a spot reading can't tell you that. Personal electronic monitors can, but at a cost that rules them out for every contract worker.

80 ppm·hr

India, Factories Act: 10 ppm averaged over an 8-hour shift.

8 ppm·hr

ACGIH (stricter, international): 1 ppm averaged over 8 hours. DoseLoop flags against both.

0.1–1 ppm

Typical shift average in field studies, even when short peaks pass 10 ppm. A spot reading misses the total.

Two scans a shift. The difference is the dose.

The card keeps darkening as long as it is worn, so one card covers many shifts. A sliding shutter closes the air holes off-shift, and the app checks that nothing changed in between.

  1. Open and scan

    At shift start, slide the shutter open (green dot shows) and scan the card with any phone.

  2. Work the shift

    H₂S diffuses through 1.0 mm holes and a 2 mm air gap into the ink, which darkens with the dose.

  3. Scan again

    At shift end, scan again. The app shows the shift dose with a range, for example 6.5 ± 0.8 ppm·hr, Safe.

  4. Close the shutter

    Slide it closed (red dot). If the next start scan differs from this end scan, the app flags off-shift exposure.

Shift dose = end scan − start scan The card is replaced by the dock when it reaches 80% of its range, when its reference cell drifts, if the heat dot has tripped, or if it was tampered with.

From a card on the wrist to an audit report

The card does the sensing with no power. A phone or the gate dock takes the photo, the scan engine runs on the device, and the app keeps the shift log offline. Every card's colours and calibration come from the factory in its signed QR.

One dock at the gate changes cards for the whole shift.

The worker lays the band in the dock and closes the lid. The dock reads the old card one last time, peels a fresh one from a sealed magazine and pushes it in. Old cards drop into a sealed bin as dose evidence.

One card change, about 20 seconds (estimate)

  1. Band in the nest, lid closed. Motors run only when the lid switch confirms it is shut.
  2. The camera reads the old card a final time under fixed 5000 K light.
  3. A NEMA 17 stepper pushes the bottom card out of the 25-card magazine.
  4. A peel bar and pinch rollers strip its foil on the way.
  5. The new card pushes the old one out into the sealed bin.
  6. The camera reads the new card's QR, heat dot and reference cell, and links it to the worker.

Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W + Camera Module 3, about 160 × 295 × 200 mm. Prototype parts about ₹8,000–15,000.

A phone does the lab work.

The web app and Android app run the full scan pipeline on the phone, offline. Every step is shown on screen so a supervisor can see why a reading was accepted or rejected.

  1. Find the 4 corner markers and flatten the photo.
  2. Decode the QR and check its signature. Fakes are rejected.
  3. Sample each patch from many pixels; reject glare and clipping.
  4. Correct the colours using the printed browns and colour patches.
  1. Self-test on a held-out brown. Too far off means retake.
  2. Strip minus reference cell, read against the scale, gives ppm·hr with a ± range.
  3. Check shutter dots, heat dot, card age and capacity used.
  4. Log worker, shift, time, dose and card serial. Alert if over limit.

"We don't trust the printer. We measure every roll, and the app uses the measured colours."

Printed colours vary with ink, paper and press speed, and phone cameras vary with light. Four layers keep the reading honest.

Measure, then encode

A spectrophotometer measures the real colour of each roll's printed scale. Those measured values go into the card's QR.

Reject bad prints

A camera checks every card on the roll. Cards more than about 2 ΔE from the roll average are thrown out.

Non-linear colour correction

The QR's colour modules plus the 6 browns correct the photo with a thin-plate-spline fit, not just white balance. Published work cut error from 90% to 14% this way.

Read it relative

Dose comes from strip minus reference cell, the same ink printed in the same pass, so thickness and humidity effects mostly cancel.

Lab tests planned pending

  • Dose calibration at 5+ levels from about 1 to 240 ppm·hr in a sealed chamber with certified H₂S gas. Target: within ±20% of a calibrated meter.
  • Same dose, different paths: 2 ppm × 8 h must read the same as 16 ppm × 1 h.
  • Detection limit about 1 ppm·hr; humidity, heat and shelf-life tests on the reference cell and heat dot.

Next, after the lab results

  • Colour JAB Code decoding in the app, and thin-plate-spline correction with the measured colours
  • A high-dose lane or a stain-length lane, only if tests show the single lane saturates or colour still varies
  • PESO/ATEX approval for hazardous zones, and a pilot in one MRPL unit
  • Cloud sync and multi-site dashboards; app in English, Hindi and Kannada with voice prompts

Six versions, each one fixing the last

Every redesign removed a real weakness: a thrown-away band, no off-shift control, a lid that could snag on machinery, then an expensive moulded pod.

  1. v1Concept

    Disposable strap with a layered sensor

    • UV window, diffusion cap, PTFE membrane, air-gap spacer
    • Sensing strip read by colour and stain length, with a reference cell
    • Printed colour scale, expiry indicator, QR and corner markers, foil skin barrier

    Weakness: the whole band was thrown away.

  2. v2First CAD model

    Reusable band, clip-in pod

    • First Fusion 360 model: flat band and a separate sensor pod
    • Strip stack, reference scale, expiry patch and markers on the pod
    • Exploded view of the layers

    Weakness: no way to stop the pod sensing between shifts.

  3. v3–v4Cradle and lid

    One pod for many shifts

    • Buckle strap and a cradle with rails, lips and a lid to close the pod off-shift
    • Disposable pod: sensing window, reference cell, colour QR, brown scale, expiry wick, tamper tab, peel-off foil
    • Scanned at the start and end of every shift, about 30 days per pod

    Weakness: a hinged lid could snag on machinery.

  4. v5Flush shutter pod

    No lid, a sliding shutter

    • Clear hole-shutter slides 1.5 mm, green and red dots show open or closed
    • 1.0 mm holes over both windows; carbon/ZnO filter dots in the reference cell
    • FKM straps, orange breakaway link, 316L quick-release, antistatic cradle

    Weakness: an 11-part moulded pod at ₹20–46 each.

  5. v6Current design

    Laminated card in a reusable frame

    • One-time card, 28.6 × 40 × 2.7 mm, made on rolls for ₹4–12
    • Shutter, rails and markers move to a frame that lasts a year
    • Heat dot replaces the dye-wick; card retired on its condition, not a date
    • Measured print colours stored in the QR

    Result: ₹15–35 per worker per month, down from ₹30–65.

  6. v6New: the dock

    Motorised card changer

    • Stepper pusher and foil peeler feed cards from a sealed 25-card magazine
    • Pi Zero 2 W and Camera Module 3 read each card under fixed light
    • Old cards kept in a sealed bin as dose evidence

    Result: no hand-handling of cards, and a better reading than a phone.
